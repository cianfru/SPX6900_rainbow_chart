Site direction & identity

A research terminal wearing a landing page's clothes.

The site is named after one chart and contains sixty-four. Its content already looks like an instrument — dark, dense, monospaced. Its shell still looks like a launch announcement. Closing that gap is the whole brief, and it also answers the logo.

Diagnosis

What the site outgrew

Five observations from the live pages, ordered by how much each one costs you.

Scope

The name describes 1/64th of the product

"SPX6900 Rainbow Chart" is accurate about the home page and wrong about everything else: 51 SPX charts across five groups, 13 more under Project Aeon, a control panel, a post engine. A visitor who wants cost-basis distribution or trader P&L has no reason to believe this site has it.

Colour

Five accent systems compete for the same eye

Gradient headline text, per-group dot colours, tinted tile borders, ambient purple/green/orange page glows, and the nine band colours inside the charts — all at once. In a chart, colour is data. Every decorative use of colour in the chrome taxes the only place colour needs to mean something.

Navigation

Browse-only IA on a research tool

Forty-one tiles in one scroll, no search, no filter, no recents, no way to ask "what changed today". The live previews are the best thing on the site — they deserve a system that can find them.

Voice

The shell undercuts the rigour of the content

A tri-colour gradient headline flanked by two kraken emoji sits directly above a power-law regression with a published R². The work is the most methodologically honest thing in this corner of crypto; the packaging reads like every other memecoin site.

Type

Two undecided typefaces

The site sets Space Grotesk; the cards render DejaVu Serif (a deliberate call from July). Neither is wrong. Having both, unreconciled, across the two surfaces people actually see is the problem.

Direction

Terminal, not landing page

Not a restyle — a change of category. The site stops presenting a chart and starts behaving like an instrument you check. Four moves, in order of impact per hour spent.

Move one · Ration the rainbow

The rainbow appears in exactly three places: the mark, the rainbow chart itself, and the card stripe. Everywhere else the chrome goes monochrome with a single accent. Scarcity is what turns a decoration into a signature — this document uses it three times, and it still reads as the loudest thing here.

Move two · Monospace the chrome

Labels, nav, metrics, eyebrows, freshness, axis readouts in mono; the sans is for prose only. This single change does most of the work of "terminal" — it is what separates an instrument from a brochure, and it costs one token swap in chart-ui.jsx.

Move three · Home becomes Today

The landing page answers "where does SPX sit right now, and what moved" — price, band, the valuation composite, today's signals, data freshness — with the rainbow as the first chart below it, not the whole page. You have already built every one of those feeds; they have simply never been assembled onto one screen.

Move four · Make 64 charts findable

A persistent left rail grouped as today's five categories plus Aeon, a / command palette over chart titles and descriptions, and recently viewed. The gallery stays — it becomes the browse view, not the only view.

The mark

Four candidates, judged at the size they'll live at

A mark for this product has to survive three hostile contexts: a 16px favicon, a circular X avatar at 48px, and the corner of a chart card viewed as a feed thumbnail. Each option below is rendered at those sizes rather than large, because large is where every mark looks fine.

A · The stripe Recommended

Promotes brandStripe — already the signature on all 74 cards — to the mark itself. Site, cards, favicon and avatar become one asset overnight, at zero migration cost. It is the only candidate still legible at 16px.

B · The ladder

The band-history bars from the home page, abstracted. Reads as measurement rather than weather, which suits the honesty position — but it loses two bands and most of its structure below 24px.

C · The arc

Today's nav icon, tidied. It is the most literal reading of "rainbow" and the most generic — concentric arcs are the default crypto-rainbow mark, and it fills at 16px.

D · The curve

Price rising through its band on a log axis — the actual thesis of the product. Strong at 48px, but at 16px it is an unidentifiable diagonal, and every trading product on earth owns a rising line.

Lockup

Stripe plus mono wordmark. The descriptor is set in the same face at lower contrast, so the lockup can shed it for the avatar and keep its proportions.

The rule

Colour in the chrome must encode state, never brand. A coloured thing on this site means something: a band, a chain, a series, good/warn/bad. If it means nothing, it is grey. This one rule prevents the drift that produced five competing accent systems, and it is enforceable in review because it is falsifiable.

The name

The decision I can't make for you

"Rainbow" carries real equity: the handle, the domain, the mark, three years of posts. It also names one chart out of sixty-four. Those facts pull in opposite directions, and the resolution is a judgement about how you want to be known.

My recommendation is to keep the word and change what it names. Rainbow becomes the brand — the mark, the handle, the stripe — and the product it names is the terminal, not the chart. The rainbow chart becomes the flagship exhibit inside it. In practice that is a one-line change to the title and subtitle, plus the shell above: no lost equity, no migration, and the site stops under-selling itself.

The alternative — a new terminal-flavoured name with Rainbow demoted to a chart — buys accuracy and costs the handle, the domain and the recognition. I do not think the accuracy is worth that, given the mark can do the work instead.

Mark

A stripe, B ladder, C arc, or D curve — and whether the gold accent survives contact with the band palette on real pages.

Recommend · A, with B as the app tile

Typography

Mono-forward chrome across site and cards, and whether the cards' DejaVu Serif stays as the card voice or converges with the site.

Recommend · mono chrome, serif stays on cards

Naming

Recommend · keep, re-scope

Once those are settled the build order is: tokens file → shell and rail → Today page → gallery search → mark and favicon set. The first two are where the change becomes visible; everything after is mechanical.
