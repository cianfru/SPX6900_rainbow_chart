# Design Principles

**Everything visible must correspond to measurable blockchain data.**

**If two visualizations preserve the same truth, prefer the one that is easier to read.**

**Compress spacing, never ordering.**

**Reality belongs in geometry; information belongs in light.**

**If something is fictional, label it explicitly.**

***

These are not aspirations written after the fact. Each one was paid for.

The third exists because holdings span nearly 3,000× and no honest linear scale can show that — so
the [height curve](reading-a-building/height.md) compresses the gaps between buildings while keeping
their order exact.

The fourth exists because tinting whole facades by data made the city look worse *and* say less. Age
and flow moved into the [windows and street glow](reading-a-building/colour.md), and both improved.

The fifth exists because a wallet's [district](reading-the-city/districts.md) is derived from a hash
of its own address. It is the one part of the city that means nothing, so it is the part labelled
most loudly.

The second is the reason a tower is never shortened because a taller one stands beside it. When the
skyline needed breathing room, the fix was moving buildings apart — not changing what they said.
