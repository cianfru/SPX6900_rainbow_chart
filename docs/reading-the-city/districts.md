# Districts

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Districts are intentionally fictional.**

They provide every wallet with a permanent address, but they carry no analytical meaning.

Only the building itself represents blockchain data.
{% endhint %}

A wallet's district comes from a hash of its own address. It is stable — you will always find
yourself in the same place — and it is arbitrary. Your neighbours are not related to you. Living in
Harlem rather than Tribeca says nothing whatsoever.

The address exists so the city can be navigated and so people can find themselves in it. That is
all it is for.

Districts are weighted by how much buildable land they actually have, so every one fills to roughly
the same density and no block is left paved and empty.

## The one exception

The very largest holders are drawn into the **tower districts**. That is what puts the skyscrapers
downtown and in Midtown rather than scattering them at random through Inwood.

So the *presence* of towers downtown is meaningful. Which particular tower stands on which particular
lot is not.

## Towers claim room

Districts fill from the core outward in conviction order, which meant ranks 1, 2, 3 and so on became
neighbours by construction — the tallest buildings in the city fused into a single dark slab over
Midtown. Not a skyline, a wall.

The fix was to give the tall cohort a clearance radius. Placement is already a game, so moving
buildings apart costs nothing true; shortening them would have cost everything.

| Rank | Clearance |
| --- | --- |
| Top 8 | 5.2 units — a block of sky each |
| To rank 40 | 3.6 |
| To rank 120 | 2.6 — roughly the glass cohort |
| To rank 320 | 1.8 |
| Everyone else | packs normally — the mass **is** the city |

Blocks, avenues and streets come from one grid, so the corridors you see are the corridors the
buildings actually left. Central Park is not buildable, and neither is the ground the bridge lands
on.
