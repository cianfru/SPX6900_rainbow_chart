# Infrastructure

**The harbour contains everything that isn't a holder.**

Coins locked forever become the **Statue of Liberty**.

Liquidity becomes **Governors Island**.

Cross-chain supply becomes the **Brooklyn Bridge**.

Centralized exchanges become **warehouses along the docks**.

These structures don't represent people. They represent the infrastructure supporting the ecosystem.

***

## The Statue of Liberty — the burn

**69.0M SPX**

`0x…dead` is receive-only. Coins sent there can never move again, by definition.

The one statue on earth everybody recognises from its outline happens to be holding up a flame. It
stands alone on Liberty Island, where the real one stands.

## Governors Island — the Uniswap pool

**13.7M SPX**

SPX launched as a DEX token, so the liquidity pool is the oldest thing in the harbour. Giving it its
own island says that.

## The Brooklyn Bridge — the Wormhole bridge

**111.3M SPX**

This is the supply that actually backs SPX on Base and Solana. The span runs the real Brooklyn
Bridge's line, Manhattan to Brooklyn, and its thickness carries the number.

## The docks off Red Hook — the exchanges

**162.9M SPX across 16 venues**

One warehouse per exchange, with its footprint sized by that venue's share of exchange-held supply.
Red Hook is New York's real container port.

Kraken's berth dwarfs everything else at **36%** of exchange-held supply, while Binance and Coinbase
hold almost nothing between them. That is the finding, not a rendering accident — and it is
counterintuitive enough to be worth the walk down the pier.

***

## Why they look like that

The warehouses are deliberately squat and wide. Nothing in the harbour should be mistaken for a
holder's building, because none of those coins belong to a person.

For scale: residents hold **643.1M** between them. The harbour is roughly a quarter of the supply —
a quarter that a holder map alone would simply lose.
